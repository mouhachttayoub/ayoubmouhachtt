Situation / Context
Start with the business problem.

A profitable single-store operation that needed a more durable growth system.

The starting business generated about $18K per month from one store, but the model was concentrated in a single product category, traffic source and ad account.

The goal was not just to improve ads. It was to reduce structural risk by creating a portfolio approach with separated acquisition systems and lifecycle revenue underneath them.

The architecture expanded to four stores with isolated account structures, diversified channels and unified reporting.

Diagnosis
Find the leak before buying more traffic.

The ceiling was structural, not creative.

01

Single point of failure

One store, one category and one primary channel created concentration risk. A supplier problem or fatigue cycle could hit the whole operation.

02

No lifecycle engine

There was little systematic work on abandoned carts, post-purchase upsells, win-back or cross-store customer value.

03

Weak capital separation

Different products and categories competed inside shared acquisition structures, making clean performance allocation harder.

Architecture
The system built.

Four stores. Separate signals. One reporting layer.

01

Isolated Meta architecture

Each store ran its own acquisition system to keep product signal and budget allocation cleaner.

02

Google Shopping

Physical stores used optimized feeds and Shopping campaigns segmented by product economics.

03

Klaviyo lifecycle

Seven automated flows covered abandonment, post-purchase, win-back and cross-store conversion.

04

AOV optimization

Bundles, thresholds and one-click post-purchase offers increased revenue per customer without requiring more traffic.

Signal / Learning
What the case actually proved.

E-commerce scale is an architecture problem as much as a traffic problem.

Adding more traffic to a fragile system creates more exposure to the same structural risks. Diversification, lifecycle and clean measurement make the operation more resilient.

The strongest efficiency gain was not a new audience. It was extracting more value from existing demand through better AOV, email automation and channel separation.

“Your AOV is revenue you are leaving on the table every day.”

Growth has a ceiling when the architecture underneath it stays fragile.

If the business already has demand but scaling feels unstable, I can help find whether the constraint is acquisition, lifecycle, offer economics or measurement.
