@@ creative-angle-matrix
META: Build an ad creative angle matrix from buying triggers, objections and proof, with a practical example that creates genuinely different concepts.
ANSWER: A creative angle matrix connects different buying reasons to a promise, demonstration and next step. Changing a headline's wording does not create a new angle. Start with the situation that makes someone care, then show why the offer fits that situation.
RELATED: creative-portfolio-coverage,customer-objection-research,ad-creative-brief-template

## Choose the buying reason before the format

An angle is a reason to evaluate the offer. “Video” and “carousel” are formats. “Finish a client report when the usual owner is absent” is a buying situation. The same situation can support several formats, but those assets still test related ideas.

Collect customer questions from permitted support records, interviews and sales conversations. Distinguish words customers actually used from interpretations. Do not manufacture urgency because the brief needs another column.

## Build a matrix the designer can use

| Buying situation | Promise to investigate | Evidence needed |
| A task takes too many steps | A simpler route to the same outcome | A complete workflow demonstration |
| A handover causes delays | Another teammate can complete the task | A role-based demonstration |
| Switching feels risky | The migration path is understandable | Real prerequisites and migration steps |
| Costs are unpredictable | The buyer can estimate the bill | A pricing example with assumptions |

Only use promises the product supports. An angle without evidence is an unanswered research question, not ready-to-publish copy.

## Worked example: four ads, one idea

Illustrative example: a team produces four reporting-tool ads saying “save time,” “work faster,” “less manual work” and “hours back.” The language changes, but the buying reason barely does. Replace two concepts with continuity during staff absence and confidence in the final report, if the product can substantiate them.

Keep a record of the situation, audience, proof and intended outcome for each asset. Evaluate downstream customer quality as well as response. A broad speed promise may attract more clicks while a specific handover demonstration brings fewer but more suitable buyers.

## Avoid a matrix too large to test

Do not multiply every audience by every angle, format and hook before checking production capacity. Select the uncertainty that matters most. If nobody understands the product, test the demonstration before fine-tuning a closing sentence.

The next brief should name one situation, one supported promise and one piece of proof. Use the [creative portfolio guide](creative-portfolio-coverage.html) to see which customer questions are still missing from the whole asset collection.

@@ ad-storyboarding-demonstration
META: Storyboard a product demonstration ad with the customer task, visible proof and necessary conditions, using a practical scene-by-scene worksheet.
ANSWER: A product demonstration storyboard should show a recognizable task, the action the product performs and a believable result. Explain prerequisites that affect the promise. The viewer should understand what happened without relying on an exaggerated voiceover.
RELATED: video-ad-test-plan,before-after-ad-claims,ad-to-landing-page-message-match

## Choose one task the viewer recognizes

Demonstrating ten features usually leaves little room to understand any one of them. Select the task connected to the advertised buying situation: compare products, prepare a report or organize incoming inquiries. Specify the starting state and what the customer needs before the demonstration works.

Use real product screens or accurately labeled mockups. Do not imply that a simulated interface is live product behavior. Hide personal information without obscuring the steps needed to understand the demonstration.

## Give every scene a job

| Scene | What to show | Question it answers |
| Situation | The existing task or obstacle | Why does this matter? |
| Starting point | Inputs and prerequisites | What does the user need? |
| Action | The relevant product workflow | How does it work? |
| Outcome | The actual output or changed state | What did the action produce? |
| Next step | A matching demo, page or offer | What can I do now? |

The sequence is a planning aid, not a required duration or universal performance formula. Shorten or expand it according to the task.

## Worked example: reporting without a magic transformation

Illustrative example: a reporting product imports a supported data file and creates an export. Show the file format, import action and final report. If the demonstration omits a lengthy manual cleanup, say so or include the necessary step. Editing should make the workflow understandable rather than conceal work that changes the promise.

The call to action can invite viewers to see the full workflow. The landing page should then explain compatibility and setup, instead of introducing an unrelated offer. Check the continuity between the last scene and the destination.

## Review before production

Read the storyboard without the voiceover. Can someone identify the customer's task and result? Then read the voiceover without the visuals. Does it claim more than the demonstration proves? Repair either mismatch before filming or recording.

Confirm claim sources, asset permissions and the production owner. Give the editor a list of details that must remain visible. For a test involving several opening scenes, keep the rest of the demonstration comparable using the [video test plan](video-ad-test-plan.html).

@@ before-after-ad-claims
META: Make before-and-after marketing claims credible by defining comparable conditions, dates, denominators and limits without inventing performance proof.
ANSWER: A before-and-after claim needs comparable conditions and an accurate explanation of what changed. A difference between two screenshots does not prove the product caused it. Define the population, period, metric and other changes before turning a result into advertising proof.
RELATED: proof-library-permissions,marketing-attribution-vs-incrementality,marketing-proof-and-trust

## Decide what the comparison actually shows

Distinguish an interface demonstration, a workflow comparison and a business-outcome claim. Showing fewer clicks in a task is different from claiming higher profit. An illustrative demonstration must be labeled as such; it should not resemble a fabricated customer dashboard.

For actual results, retain original records and permission to publish. Keep the metric definition stable. Revenue before refunds and collected revenue after refunds are not interchangeable measures.

## Check comparability

| Condition | Question before publication |
| Population | Are the same kinds of customers included? |
| Window | Are periods comparable and outcomes mature? |
| Offer | Did price, discount or availability change? |
| Acquisition | Did budget or audience mix change? |
| Metric | Is the definition identical in both periods? |
| Attribution | What supports the claimed cause? |

If these conditions differ, explain the difference. A useful case study can report uncertainty instead of presenting a clean causal headline.

## Worked example: a bigger total is not a conversion lift

Illustrative example: orders rise from 100 to 150 while visits rise from 2,000 to 3,000. Conversion remains 5% in both periods. Advertising “50% higher conversion” would be wrong. “Orders increased by 50%” is arithmetically correct but still needs context about the higher traffic and any other changes.

Likewise, a smaller time measurement is only comparable if the starting task and conditions are comparable. Do not time a prepared demonstration against a first-time user struggling with an unfamiliar tool.

## Preserve limitations in the creative

Essential conditions belong where the audience can understand them. A headline should remain accurate without relying on hidden fine print. Avoid presenting selected best outcomes as typical customer expectations.

Write the claim, its supporting record and a plain-language limitation before designing the ad. If evidence is insufficient, demonstrate the mechanism instead of manufacturing a result. The [proof library guide](proof-library-permissions.html) helps keep permitted evidence traceable.

@@ creative-portfolio-coverage
META: Audit your ad creative portfolio by customer question, evidence and buying stage, then identify gaps before commissioning more assets.
ANSWER: Creative portfolio coverage asks whether your assets answer different customer questions. Count distinct demonstrations, objections and buying situations, not just files. A folder with many ads can still contain only one supported idea.
RELATED: creative-angle-matrix,customer-objection-research,creative-production-capacity

## Inventory the meaning of each asset

Record the intended audience situation, question, promise, evidence and next step. Ignore file names such as “final-v7” when judging coverage. Several sizes of the same asset are useful for placement, but they do not create several learning hypotheses.

Separate assets that introduce the problem from those that explain implementation or evaluate fit. Do not force every ad to answer every buying question. Give the destination page a clear role in completing the explanation.

## Use a coverage worksheet

| Customer question | Suitable evidence | Coverage check |
| What does this help me do? | A clear task demonstration | Is the result understandable? |
| Does it fit my situation? | A specific use case | Are fit boundaries visible? |
| Can I start successfully? | Prerequisites and setup | Are obstacles explained? |
| Why should I trust it? | Permitted, relevant proof | Does the proof support the claim? |
| What happens after purchase? | Onboarding or delivery steps | Does the next step match the offer? |

The worksheet identifies gaps; it does not imply that every question deserves a separate campaign or asset.

## Worked example: twenty assets with no setup explanation

Illustrative example: a SaaS team has twenty short ads about speed. None shows the integration required to produce the promised output. Prospects click, then leave when setup becomes clear. The missing asset is a realistic setup explanation, not a twenty-first version of the speed headline.

A setup demonstration may reduce unqualified clicks while improving the suitability of trial users. Evaluate activation and support questions, not only click-through rate. An asset can help the business by filtering poor fit rather than maximizing volume.

## Prioritize gaps with evidence

Use repeated customer questions and journey drop-offs to decide which missing explanation matters. If buyers already understand setup but hesitate about migration, focus there. Avoid filling the worksheet merely to make it symmetrical.

Choose one gap and specify the evidence required, production effort and intended outcome. Reassess after the asset has served enough of the relevant journey. Combine this audit with the [angle matrix](creative-angle-matrix.html) so concepts are both distinct and useful.

@@ customer-objection-research
META: Research customer objections through actual buying decisions, classify their causes and choose accurate proof instead of writing generic reassurance.
ANSWER: Customer objection research identifies what prevents a suitable buyer from taking the next step. Separate missing information, genuine product mismatch, risk and low urgency. Different barriers need different responses; reassurance alone cannot repair an unsupported capability.
RELATED: jobs-to-be-done-switch-interview,competitive-alternative-map,marketing-proof-and-trust

## Collect evidence at the decision point

Use recent sales questions, support inquiries and interviews with people who evaluated the offer. Ask what they needed to decide, what they tried to verify and what made them postpone. A broad satisfaction survey may miss the purchase obstacle.

Keep customer quotations separate from your summary. Remove identifying details from working examples and obtain permission before publishing a quote. A team assumption such as “people think we are expensive” needs evidence about compared alternatives and perceived value.

## Classify the barrier

| Barrier | Useful response | Weak response |
| Missing information | Explain the requirement or process | Repeat the headline |
| Perceived risk | Demonstrate the relevant safeguard | Promise that nothing can go wrong |
| Product mismatch | State the fit boundary accurately | Pretend the missing capability exists |
| Low urgency | Explain a real timing trigger | Invent scarcity |
| Internal approval | Provide information for the other stakeholders | Pressure the champion to buy alone |

Use the classification to choose a proof requirement. It is not a license to argue every customer into a purchase.

## Worked example: “too expensive” means different things

Illustrative example: one buyer says the tool costs too much because they need only one report a month. Another says the same phrase because implementation costs are unknown. The first may be a poor fit for the current plan; the second needs a clearer explanation of setup effort and fees.

Offering both buyers a discount can conceal these differences. The research should reveal what would make the decision easier and whether the business can provide it profitably and honestly.

## Turn evidence into a test

Choose an objection that appears repeatedly among suitable buyers. Create the smallest supported response: a migration checklist, a transparent pricing example or a demonstration of an actual workflow. Keep the underlying offer stable if you want to investigate the explanation itself.

Review qualified progression and the questions people still ask. If the new asset attracts a different audience, report that mix change rather than treating the result as a pure persuasion effect. Start with the [switch interview](jobs-to-be-done-switch-interview.html) when the objection's meaning remains unclear.
