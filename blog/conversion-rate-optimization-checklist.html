Conversion optimization

Conversion rate optimization checklist: fix the journey first

A practical CRO checklist for landing pages and stores: message match, mobile usability, evidence, checkout friction and meaningful experiments.

By Ayoub Mouhachtt3 min read
The short answerConversion rate optimization improves the path between a visitor’s intent and a useful business action. Start by locating the friction in that path; do not assume a button-color test is your biggest opportunity.

Choose the conversion and its denominator

A landing-page conversion rate might mean qualified demo requests divided by landing-page sessions. A store rate might mean orders divided by online-store sessions. These are different metrics, and a checkout completion rate uses another denominator again.

Write the definition next to the report. Segment by device, source and landing page before diagnosing a drop. A site-wide average can fall because you brought in more early-stage visitors, even if each page performs as before. Make sure a tracking change has not created the apparent problem.

Check message match above the fold

Read the ad or search promise, then open the destination as a new visitor. Can you identify the product, intended customer, relevant outcome and next action without guessing? Does the page prove the promise or merely repeat it?

Remove vague claims when a concrete explanation is available. Show what the customer receives and how it works. The first screen does not need to answer every objection, but it should orient the visitor and make the next step credible. Keep the primary action visually clear without covering the content with pop-ups.

Inspect the mobile journey with real tasks

Try completing the main action on a phone: selecting a variant, reading delivery details, filling a form and correcting an error. Watch for sticky elements covering controls, small tap targets, confusing field labels and content that shifts while loading.

A page can look attractive in a screenshot and still be difficult to use. Review slower connections and long product names. Make errors recoverable: tell the user what needs fixing and preserve information they have already entered. For forms, ask only for information needed at that stage.

Place evidence near the decision

Use proof that answers the specific uncertainty. A product demonstration explains function. Accurate dimensions explain fit. A relevant case study explains your approach. A real, authorized testimonial can help a visitor evaluate an experience. Generic badges cannot replace missing product information.

If you do not have suitable customer proof, show the process or the product clearly instead of inventing reviews. Distinguish examples from delivered work. Honest information can also discourage unsuitable purchases, reducing refunds and support burden even if the initial conversion count does not rise.

Prioritize tests by evidence and business value

Write a hypothesis in the form: “Visitors hesitate because [observed problem]; changing [specific element] should improve [metric] without hurting [guardrail].” Link the hypothesis to customer feedback, session behavior or funnel data.

Check that your traffic can support the experiment. Small samples and short windows can produce misleading winners. A usability fix may be worth implementing directly when the issue is clear; a commercial claim deserves stronger evidence. Evaluate downstream quality, refunds or activation alongside the immediate conversion. Document what changed so the next review can explain the result.

Try this next

Complete the main journey on mobile and record three obstacles. Fix the clearest obstacle, then measure the relevant funnel stage.

Turn this guide into a working brief

  1. Write down the business question and the metric that would answer it.
  2. Choose one change you can isolate and explain why it should help.
  3. Decide who owns the work, how it will be measured and when to review it.
