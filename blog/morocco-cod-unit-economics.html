Morocco COD unit economics: calculate contribution from delivered orders

Model cash-on-delivery economics with delivered revenue, dispatch and return costs, product costs and media spending, using explicit assumptions.

By Ayoub Mouhachtt3 min read7 October 2026AI-assisted guide
The short answerCash-on-delivery unit economics should follow collected revenue and the costs of both successful and unsuccessful orders. A created order is not yet revenue received. For a Moroccan store using COD, define confirmation, dispatch, delivery and collection separately before deciding whether advertising is supportable.

Write the operational cost scope

Include product cost, packaging, outbound delivery, failed-delivery or return charges and media spending as relevant. Specify how reusable returned inventory is treated. Accounting, cash flow and marketing contribution can use different views, so make the purpose explicit.

Use actual agreements and operational records for the business. The example below is a teaching model, not a market benchmark or a claim about courier prices.

Worked example: a full cohort

Illustrative example: 100 dispatched orders have a selling price of 200 MAD. Seventy deliver and collect, producing 14,000 MAD. Assume product cost assigned to delivered orders is 70 × 80 = 5,600 MAD, outbound delivery is 100 × 20 = 2,000 MAD, packaging is 100 × 5 = 500 MAD, return fees are 30 × 10 = 300 MAD and media spending is 3,000 MAD.

Under those stated assumptions, contribution after these costs is 14,000 − 5,600 − 2,000 − 500 − 300 − 3,000 = 2,600 MAD. Returned products are assumed reusable in this model. Staff, tax, other fees and inventory cash timing are excluded, so the result is not accounting net profit.

Build the operating calculation

  1. Choose a mature order cohort and eligible status definitions.
  2. Reconcile delivered and collected outcomes with operational records.
  3. Assign the relevant costs under a written scope.
  4. Separate new-customer and repeat-customer economics when identifiable.
  5. Compare contribution by campaign, product or region only where records support it.

Do not allocate all costs only to delivered orders while ignoring unsuccessful attempts. Those attempts can consume media, packaging and courier resources.

COD measurement table

RecordRole
Created orderInitial demand and workload.
Confirmed orderVerified progression under the business rule.
Dispatched orderFulfillment commitment and cost exposure.
Delivered and collectedCompleted operational revenue outcome.

Inspect the growth constraint

Low contribution can come from media cost, poor confirmation, delivery problems or a weak basket. The repair depends on where the cost or loss occurs. A campaign adjustment is not the only possible intervention.

Should a store optimize cost per order form?

Use it as an early diagnostic, not the complete economics measure. The form still needs confirmation, delivery and collection.

Does every returned order lose the full product cost?

It depends on the item's condition and operational treatment. State the assumption and use the actual business records.

Put this into practice

Calculate one mature cohort from created order through collected revenue. Write every included and excluded cost beside the result before using it to scale advertising.
