Marketing and logistics alignment: make the delivery promise match reality

Align ads, product pages, confirmation and delivery operations so customers receive consistent expectations and growth does not exceed fulfillment capacity.

By Ayoub Mouhachtt2 min read7 October 2026AI-assisted guide
The short answerMarketing and logistics alignment keeps the advertised promise consistent with the delivery process. For COD commerce, this includes service coverage, timing, stock, order details and exception handling. More demand is not healthy growth when the business cannot fulfill what the customer was led to expect.

Share the conditions before launch

Marketing needs current stock, supported regions and realistic delivery expectations. Operations needs to know which products, offers and demand volumes the campaign may create. A launch brief should include both sets of information.

Avoid promises based on the best possible delivery case when the target audience faces a different normal condition. Communicate meaningful limitations before the customer commits.

Build the handoff

  1. Confirm product and variant availability.
  2. Agree on coverage and delivery conditions.
  3. Carry verified order details into fulfillment.
  4. Define who handles address changes, delays and complaints.
  5. Feed refusal and delivery evidence back into the marketing review.

The feedback should lead to a specific repair: a clearer image, an adjusted offer or an operational change. “Customers are bad” is not an actionable diagnosis.

Alignment table

TeamInformation to share
MarketingAudience, promise, offer and expected demand.
ConfirmationVerified intent, details and unresolved questions.
FulfillmentStock, dispatch rules and service capacity.
SupportExceptions and recurring customer confusion.

Worked example: an offer creates a bottleneck

Illustrative example: an advertisement promotes a bundle that requires extra packing work, but fulfillment capacity was estimated from single-item orders. Dispatch slows and customers become uncertain. The response may include operational preparation and accurate expectations before increasing acquisition.

Changing only the advertisement's audience would not address the capacity mismatch.

Use a common operating review

Review mature delivered outcomes, unresolved exceptions and contribution. Keep the source definitions shared so each team is not reporting a different version of “successful order.” Add a visible note when conditions change.

Should marketing report logistics failures?

It should include relevant operational outcomes in the business view while keeping ownership clear. Reporting only initial demand hides part of the customer journey.

Can better communication solve every delivery issue?

No. Accurate messages help expectations, but inventory, routing and execution problems require operational fixes.

Put this into practice

Before the next campaign, write the delivery promise and ask the operational owner to verify it. Add a feedback route for any customer expectation the campaign creates incorrectly.
