COD order dashboard: show progression, pending outcomes and contribution

Build a COD dashboard that separates created, confirmed, dispatched, delivered and collected orders, with mature cohorts and explicit cost definitions.

By Ayoub Mouhachtt2 min read7 October 2026AI-assisted guide
The short answerA COD order dashboard should show how demand progresses into completed delivery and collection. Separate initial orders, confirmation, dispatch and final outcomes, then connect them to contribution. The reader needs to see pending work and outcome maturity rather than a single attractive order total.

Define the dashboard's decision

A campaign operator may need source quality; an operations owner may need unresolved deliveries; a founder may need contribution and cash timing. Use a shared definition layer while giving each reader the view required for their job.

Keep operational order totals separate from advertising-platform credit. They describe different records and should not be added together.

Build the views

  1. Funnel counts under consistent eligible definitions.
  2. Mature cohort outcomes at comparable ages.
  3. Pending and exception states needing action.
  4. Costs and contribution under a stated scope.
  5. Segments that explain meaningful differences in decisions.

Show counts beside rates so small groups are not mistaken for stable patterns. Make the denominator visible for each percentage.

Dashboard table

PanelQuestion
Created and confirmedWhere does initial intent fail to progress?
Dispatched and deliveredWhat happens after fulfillment begins?
Pending and exceptionsWhat still needs an owner?
Collected revenue and contributionWhat value remains under the cost definition?

Worked example: unfinished versus failed

Illustrative example: a new cohort has many pending deliveries, while an older cohort is mostly resolved. A dashboard comparing their delivery rates without age notes may imply a decline. Use equivalent observation windows or label the new cohort partial.

An operations view can still show the pending workload immediately. A marketing profitability view should not treat unresolved orders as final outcomes.

Make the report actionable

Name the owner for every exception panel and state the next action. A dashboard that identifies delays but routes nothing can become a display rather than an operating tool. Review whether the information actually changes decisions.

Should the main headline be order volume?

It can show demand, but the business view also needs completion and economics. A growing initial count can hide weaker delivery quality.

Can one dashboard serve every team?

Shared definitions help, while separate views can preserve clarity. Avoid a screen crowded with every possible metric.

Put this into practice

Add the cohort age, pending count and cost scope to the current report. Remove any percentage whose denominator the team cannot explain consistently.
