E-commerce offer design: make the purchase worthwhile without hiding the cost

Design an e-commerce offer around customer fit, product value, transparent conditions and contribution economics rather than an automatic discount.

By Ayoub Mouhachtt3 min read7 October 2026AI-assisted guide
The short answerAn e-commerce offer combines the product, price, inclusions, purchase conditions and reason to act. Improving the offer does not always mean lowering the price. A suitable bundle, clearer delivery terms or better proof can make evaluation easier while protecting contribution and customer trust.

Identify the buying obstacle

Separate an affordability concern from a value or confidence concern. Buyers may hesitate because they do not understand the product, cannot judge quality or discover shipping cost late. A discount may temporarily increase orders without resolving those questions.

Look at customer messages, returns and refusal reasons. These records can reveal whether the offer attracts people who are disappointed by what actually arrives or by the work required to use it.

Build the offer deliberately

  1. Define the customer task and the product combination that serves it.
  2. State exactly what is included and what must be obtained separately.
  3. Make delivery, digital access, compatibility and other important conditions visible.
  4. Calculate contribution after variable costs and proposed incentives.
  5. Measure completed customer outcomes, not just checkout starts.

Avoid adding bonuses that increase clutter without improving the customer's task. More items can make an offer harder to understand.

Compare offer changes

ChangeQuestion to answer
DiscountDoes added demand compensate for lower contribution?
BundleDo the items serve a coherent task together?
Shipping thresholdDoes the basket behavior justify the fulfillment cost?
Clearer previewDoes it improve confidence and reduce misunderstandings?

Worked example: contribution after a discount

Illustrative example: an order priced at 100 units has 60 units of variable cost before advertising. A 10-unit discount reduces pre-advertising contribution from 40 to 30 units if the other costs remain unchanged. More orders must be evaluated against that reduced contribution, not celebrated from revenue alone.

The calculation does not establish whether the discount is good or bad. It shows the economic question the test must answer.

Protect expectation quality

Do not imply that demonstration props, printing materials or unlisted services are included. For digital products, explain delivery format and required tools. A clear offer can reduce unsuitable purchases even if it produces fewer initial clicks.

Are bundles always better than single products?

No. A bundle helps when the items solve a related task and the buyer values the combination. Unrelated extras can create confusion.

Does urgency improve an offer?

A real deadline or limited stock can be relevant. Invented scarcity weakens trust and should not replace a clear value explanation.

Put this into practice

Write your offer as product, inclusions, conditions and customer outcome. Then calculate the contribution effect of one proposed change before running it.
