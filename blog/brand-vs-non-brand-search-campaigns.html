Brand vs non-brand search campaigns: report demand capture separately

Separate branded and non-branded search reporting so existing awareness, new demand, customer quality and incrementality are easier to evaluate.

By Ayoub Mouhachtt3 min read7 October 2026AI-assisted guide
The short answerBranded searches include a business or product name; non-branded searches describe a problem, category or alternative without that specific name. Separate their reporting because they can represent different levels of prior awareness. A blended acquisition metric can hide how much performance comes from people already looking for you.

Identify the job of each campaign

A branded campaign may help a searcher reach the intended offer or current destination. A non-branded campaign may introduce the business to people considering a problem or category. Both can produce valuable outcomes, but they should not automatically receive the same acquisition interpretation.

Also inspect existing-customer intent. Login, support or account-management searches can resemble brand demand while serving a different business task. Send those visitors to suitable destinations rather than forcing a sales journey.

Separate the reporting

Search typeMain interpretation question
Brand with purchase intentWhat role does the ad play for people already aware of the offer?
Brand with support intentIs the visitor reaching the correct service destination?
Category or problemAre suitable new customers discovering and evaluating the offer?
Competitor or alternativeCan the business make an honest, relevant case for fit?

Evaluate without assumptions

  1. Classify searches using actual terms, not only campaign names.
  2. Distinguish new and existing customer outcomes where reliable data allows it.
  3. Compare qualification and conversion lag across groups.
  4. Consider the organic result and other discovery paths.
  5. Use a suitable controlled test if incremental impact is the unresolved question.

Do not assume every branded conversion would disappear without the advertisement, or that every one would occur organically. Those are different hypotheses that need evidence.

Worked example: a flattering blended number

Illustrative example: a campaign report combines 40 branded conversions with 10 non-branded conversions. Most of the visible efficiency may come from known-brand demand. Separate costs and outcomes before deciding whether the program is expanding the customer base or mainly serving existing awareness.

The distinction does not make branded activity worthless. It helps assign it an appropriate purpose and prevents a broad “new customer acquisition” claim unsupported by the report.

Watch for mixed queries

A query containing both a brand and a product category can still require evaluation. Review the intent rather than mechanically treating every mention as identical. Keep the classification documented so reporting remains consistent over time.

Should branded ads always be paused?

No universal rule applies. Consider customer intent, organic visibility, auction conditions and measured incremental value.

Can non-branded traffic include existing customers?

Yes. Search wording does not identify a person's complete history. Use customer records carefully where available and acknowledge remaining uncertainty.

Put this into practice

Split your recent search outcomes into branded, non-branded and service-intent groups. Write the business job and success measure for each before reallocating spending.
