Bilingual marketing copy review: preserve the promise across languages

Review bilingual marketing copy for meaning, price, eligibility and layout so English, French and Arabic versions make the same accurate promise.

Ayoub’s Growth Notebook2 min readPrepared 13 October 2026AI-assisted guide
The short answerBilingual copy review should preserve the offer and claim strength, not translate every phrase literally. Check meaning, conditions, price and next step across languages. Then review the actual layout, especially when Arabic and Latin text appear together.

Write an offer reference before translation

Record the product, intended buyer, supported outcome, price, exclusions and action. This becomes the factual reference for every language. Separate facts that cannot change from tone that can be localized.

English brevity may require a different sentence shape in French or Arabic. A natural adaptation is useful; silently changing “can help” into “guarantees” is not. Keep dates, currencies and units explicit.

Use a bilingual review table

ItemReview question
PromiseDoes each version claim the same outcome?
ConditionsAre prerequisites and exclusions retained?
PriceAre currency, billing interval and fees clear?
ActionDoes the button lead to the corresponding next step?
ToneDoes the wording fit the audience without changing facts?
LayoutAre numbers, links and reading direction understandable?

Ask a fluent reviewer to explain the offer back in their own words. That checks understanding better than comparing words line by line.

Worked example: one consultation, three meanings

Illustrative example: English copy advertises one paid consultation, French copy accidentally suggests ongoing support, and Arabic copy omits that payment comes before booking. The design may look consistent while the offer differs. Repair the scope and sequence before publishing.

For mixed-direction layouts, keep a currency amount or URL together and inspect how it appears alongside Arabic. Do not rely on visual punctuation alone to communicate an important condition.

Test the whole journey

Review the advertisement, destination page, checkout and confirmation together. A language-specific ad can still send customers to an unexplained checkout in another language. State any unavoidable switch clearly where it affects understanding.

Use the same source record for future changes. If pricing or booking steps change, update every actual language version rather than assuming the translator's old copy remains correct. The multilingual confirmation script provides a practical example of preserving one promise.
