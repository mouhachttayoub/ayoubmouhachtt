Average order value vs profit: bigger baskets can still create weaker economics

Compare average order value with contribution after product, fulfillment, discounts and other variable costs before evaluating an upsell or bundle as a win.

By Ayoub Mouhachtt2 min read7 October 2026AI-assisted guide
The short answerAverage order value describes revenue per order under a defined reporting measure. It is not profit. A larger basket can include higher costs, deeper discounts or more returns. Evaluate contribution and customer quality alongside order value when judging bundles, upsells or shipping thresholds.

Define the revenue measure

State whether the order value includes taxes, shipping, discounts and adjustments according to the reporting system. Different tools can use different definitions. Keep the operational contribution calculation separate and explain the cost categories included.

Also distinguish orders from customers. A change in average order value does not show how many people bought or whether they will return.

Compare useful measures

MeasureQuestion it answers
Average order valueHow much reported revenue does an average order represent?
Contribution per orderWhat remains after the chosen variable costs?
Contribution per customerHow does value accumulate across purchases?
Return or refusal rateDoes the larger basket create poorer purchase quality?

Evaluate an offer change

  1. Record the baseline definitions and eligible order population.
  2. Calculate product and fulfillment costs for the proposed basket.
  3. Include incentives and discounts in the comparison.
  4. Watch conversion, returns and delivery outcomes.
  5. Compare total contribution, not just the order-value percentage.

An indexed chart can communicate a relative change, but label the baseline and calculation. An index does not establish the underlying monetary amounts.

Worked example: more revenue, less contribution

Illustrative example: a 100-unit order has 60 units of variable cost, leaving 40 before advertising. A bundle sells for 130 but costs 95, leaving 35. Average order value rises while contribution per order falls. The business needs enough additional volume or repeat value to justify that tradeoff.

The example does not prove bundles are bad. It shows why revenue alone cannot answer the economics question.

Inspect the customer experience

Unwanted extras or confusing upsells can increase refunds and support work. Offer complementary products that help the customer's task and make additions optional and understandable. The basket should be useful, not merely larger.

Is a higher average order value always desirable?

No. It must be evaluated alongside conversion volume, costs and customer outcomes.

Should advertising cost be included in contribution?

Specify the version you use. A pre-advertising contribution measure and a post-acquisition measure can both be useful when clearly labeled.

Put this into practice

Calculate contribution for your most common basket and one proposed higher-value basket. Write the condition under which the second would improve the business rather than only the revenue metric.
