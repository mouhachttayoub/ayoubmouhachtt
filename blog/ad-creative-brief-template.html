Ad creative brief template: give the designer a customer problem to solve

Write an ad creative brief with a buying situation, customer tension, single promise, credible proof and a clear learning question.

By Ayoub Mouhachtt3 min read7 October 2026AI-assisted guide
The short answerAn ad creative brief should explain the customer situation and the message the asset needs to communicate. Describe the problem, promise, proof and next step before specifying colors or formats. The designer needs a decision to support, not just a request to make something eye-catching.

Make the customer tension concrete

A tension is the gap between what the customer wants and what makes that difficult. “Busy founders” is an audience label; “a founder repeatedly chasing trial users who never complete setup” is a situation a creative team can illustrate.

Use language from real customer questions where you have permission and context. Record the source of the observation without including private customer details in a widely shared brief.

Fill the brief

  1. Audience situation: who is experiencing the problem right now?
  2. Tension: what happens if the problem remains unresolved?
  3. Promise: what specific outcome can the offer support?
  4. Proof: what demonstration or evidence can substantiate that promise?
  5. Action: what should the viewer do next, and what happens afterward?
  6. Learning question: which assumption is this creative intended to test?

Add placement requirements and safe areas after the message is clear. Technical specifications matter, but they should not become the main strategy.

Separate requirements from creative freedom

Fixed requirementRoom for exploration
Accurate product capabilityThe clearest way to demonstrate it.
Approved logo and offer conditionsComposition, pacing and visual hierarchy.
Required next stepThe supporting story or sequence.
Confidentiality boundariesWhich public proof communicates credibility.

Worked example: a stronger request

Illustrative example: instead of “make a premium ad for our software,” brief a demonstration of a store owner finding the next pending order without opening several spreadsheets. Show the workflow honestly, explain what setup is required and send viewers to the relevant use case. The test asks whether that task resonates with suitable owners.

This gives the designer an observable moment to show. It also prevents the creative from becoming a collage of unrelated features.

Review against the brief

Ask whether the audience can understand the problem, outcome and next step. Do not let personal preferences about decoration replace the communication objective. If reviewers disagree, identify which part of the brief is unresolved before adding more design revisions.

How long should a brief be?

Long enough to explain the buying situation and boundaries, short enough to use during production. Attach evidence separately if it would obscure the main direction.

Should a brief include a finished headline?

It can include a working headline, but explain the underlying message so the team can improve the wording without losing the purpose.

Put this into practice

Write the promise and proof for one asset in two sentences. If they do not connect, repair the brief before requesting another design version.
