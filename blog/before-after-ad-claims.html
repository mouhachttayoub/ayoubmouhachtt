Before-and-after marketing claims: make the comparison defensible

Make before-and-after marketing claims credible by defining comparable conditions, dates, denominators and limits without inventing performance proof.

Ayoub’s Growth Notebook2 min readPrepared 13 October 2026AI-assisted guide
The short answerA before-and-after claim needs comparable conditions and an accurate explanation of what changed. A difference between two screenshots does not prove the product caused it. Define the population, period, metric and other changes before turning a result into advertising proof.

Decide what the comparison actually shows

Distinguish an interface demonstration, a workflow comparison and a business-outcome claim. Showing fewer clicks in a task is different from claiming higher profit. An illustrative demonstration must be labeled as such; it should not resemble a fabricated customer dashboard.

For actual results, retain original records and permission to publish. Keep the metric definition stable. Revenue before refunds and collected revenue after refunds are not interchangeable measures.

Check comparability

ConditionQuestion before publication
PopulationAre the same kinds of customers included?
WindowAre periods comparable and outcomes mature?
OfferDid price, discount or availability change?
AcquisitionDid budget or audience mix change?
MetricIs the definition identical in both periods?
AttributionWhat supports the claimed cause?

If these conditions differ, explain the difference. A useful case study can report uncertainty instead of presenting a clean causal headline.

Worked example: a bigger total is not a conversion lift

Illustrative example: orders rise from 100 to 150 while visits rise from 2,000 to 3,000. Conversion remains 5% in both periods. Advertising “50% higher conversion” would be wrong. “Orders increased by 50%” is arithmetically correct but still needs context about the higher traffic and any other changes.

Likewise, a smaller time measurement is only comparable if the starting task and conditions are comparable. Do not time a prepared demonstration against a first-time user struggling with an unfamiliar tool.

Preserve limitations in the creative

Essential conditions belong where the audience can understand them. A headline should remain accurate without relying on hidden fine print. Avoid presenting selected best outcomes as typical customer expectations.

Write the claim, its supporting record and a plain-language limitation before designing the ad. If evidence is insufficient, demonstrate the mechanism instead of manufacturing a result. The proof library guide helps keep permitted evidence traceable.
