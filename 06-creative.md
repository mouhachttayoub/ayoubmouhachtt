@@ advertorial-disclosure-conversion
META: Build an advertorial landing page that explains a product honestly, distinguishes evidence from persuasion and keeps the commercial relationship clear.
ANSWER: An advertorial is promotional content presented in an explanatory format. It can help buyers understand a problem and offer, but it should not disguise the seller or imitate independent journalism. Make the commercial relationship clear and support the product claims.

## Match the format to a buying question

Use the page to answer a question that benefits from explanation: how a workflow operates, what a migration involves or how to evaluate product fit. Do not create a fake review site merely to repeat a sales page in a supposedly independent voice.

Identify the business behind the content. If contributors are paid or evidence is supplied by the seller, describe that relationship accurately. Do not invent reporters, customer interviews or expert endorsements.

## Separate explanation, proof and offer

| Page element | What it should do |
| Commercial disclosure | Make the promotional relationship understandable |
| Problem explanation | Describe a real task and existing alternatives |
| Product demonstration | Show the actual mechanism and prerequisites |
| Evidence | Support specific claims with permitted records |
| Offer | Explain the price, scope and next step |

Where paid promotional links are involved, Google's guidance requires appropriate link qualification. This is separate from the reader's need to understand who is selling the offer.

## Worked example: a workflow article that sells transparently

Illustrative example: a software company publishes a guide to preparing client reports and explains how its own product handles supported inputs. It labels the content as product education, shows a real output and states compatibility limits. The call to action offers the complete demonstration.

The article should still be useful to someone who does not buy. Give enough evaluation criteria to recognize poor fit. Do not claim that all manual alternatives are broken or that a selected customer result represents every buyer.

## Review the page as a skeptical buyer

Can the reader identify the seller, verify the demonstration and understand the purchase? Does any editorial styling imply independence the page does not have? Check that the main promise remains accurate without fine print.

Measure qualified progression and customer questions after the click. A page that increases immediate sales but creates avoidable refunds needs review. Use the [before-and-after claims guide](before-after-ad-claims.html) when performance evidence is part of the argument.

@@ bilingual-copy-review
META: Review bilingual marketing copy for meaning, price, eligibility and layout so English, French and Arabic versions make the same accurate promise.
ANSWER: Bilingual copy review should preserve the offer and claim strength, not translate every phrase literally. Check meaning, conditions, price and next step across languages. Then review the actual layout, especially when Arabic and Latin text appear together.

## Write an offer reference before translation

Record the product, intended buyer, supported outcome, price, exclusions and action. This becomes the factual reference for every language. Separate facts that cannot change from tone that can be localized.

English brevity may require a different sentence shape in French or Arabic. A natural adaptation is useful; silently changing “can help” into “guarantees” is not. Keep dates, currencies and units explicit.

## Use a bilingual review table

| Item | Review question |
| Promise | Does each version claim the same outcome? |
| Conditions | Are prerequisites and exclusions retained? |
| Price | Are currency, billing interval and fees clear? |
| Action | Does the button lead to the corresponding next step? |
| Tone | Does the wording fit the audience without changing facts? |
| Layout | Are numbers, links and reading direction understandable? |

Ask a fluent reviewer to explain the offer back in their own words. That checks understanding better than comparing words line by line.

## Worked example: one consultation, three meanings

Illustrative example: English copy advertises one paid consultation, French copy accidentally suggests ongoing support, and Arabic copy omits that payment comes before booking. The design may look consistent while the offer differs. Repair the scope and sequence before publishing.

For mixed-direction layouts, keep a currency amount or URL together and inspect how it appears alongside Arabic. Do not rely on visual punctuation alone to communicate an important condition.

## Test the whole journey

Review the advertisement, destination page, checkout and confirmation together. A language-specific ad can still send customers to an unexplained checkout in another language. State any unavoidable switch clearly where it affects understanding.

Use the same source record for future changes. If pricing or booking steps change, update every actual language version rather than assuming the translator's old copy remains correct. The [multilingual confirmation script](multilingual-cod-call-script.html) provides a practical example of preserving one promise.

@@ video-ad-test-plan
META: Build a video ad test plan that distinguishes hooks, demonstrations and CTAs, tracks production changes and evaluates downstream customer quality.
ANSWER: A video ad test plan should name the component being changed and the customer behavior it is meant to influence. Keep a readable record of hooks, demonstrations and calls to action. Testing several full edits at once can identify a promising asset but cannot isolate which scene caused the difference.
RELATED: ad-storyboarding-demonstration,creative-testing-learning-log,creative-angle-matrix

## Choose the uncertainty you want to reduce

If suitable viewers do not understand the product, a clearer demonstration may matter more than another opening line. If they understand it but do not recognize its relevance, test a different buying situation. Let the question determine the creative change.

Do not assume every video must be the same length. Preserve the explanation required for the task. A truncated workflow can improve superficial attention while producing inaccurate expectations.

## Record the variants

| Component | What to record |
| Hook | Buying situation and opening visual |
| Demonstration | Product task and visible proof |
| CTA | Next step and destination |
| Edit changes | Duration, captions, pacing and omitted steps |
| Outcome | Qualified progression and customer safeguards |

Distinguish an isolated component test from a broader concept comparison. Both can be useful when their interpretation is honest.

## Worked example: two hooks, stable demonstration

Illustrative example: the same reporting demonstration starts with either a missed client deadline or a teammate handover. Keeping the remaining scenes and destination consistent makes the opening hypothesis easier to discuss. Audience exposure and platform delivery still need review; uploaded variants do not guarantee randomized comparison.

Record attention measures as diagnostics, not the final business outcome. More people watching the opening does not establish more suitable purchases. Compare downstream activation, qualification or contribution when those records are available and mature.

## Keep failed edits in the record

Save the hypothesis and result even when an asset is discontinued. Note whether the product changed, the destination broke or the audience differed. Otherwise the team may repeatedly produce the same concept under new filenames.

Choose a decision: keep observing, produce a revised demonstration or expand the promising concept. Use the [storyboard worksheet](ad-storyboarding-demonstration.html) to make the next production brief specific rather than asking for “a better video.”

@@ proof-library-permissions
META: Organize testimonials, dashboards and demonstrations with publication permissions, source records and confidentiality checks before using them in marketing.
ANSWER: A marketing proof library should store the original evidence, the claim it supports and permission for each intended use. Public proof is a selected, authorized view of the record. Never assume access to a dashboard means its figures can be published.
RELATED: before-after-ad-claims,marketing-proof-and-trust,agency-handover-checklist

## Keep evidence and public assets separate

Retain the original record in an appropriately restricted location. Create a publication version that removes unnecessary identifying or confidential information. Redaction should protect privacy without changing the meaning of the evidence.

Do not replace a private number with an invented public result. If a revenue figure cannot be disclosed, explain the work, process and permitted outcomes instead. A specific implementation story can be credible without revealing confidential finances.

## Store the permissions with the asset

| Field | What to retain |
| Source | Original record and date |
| Claim | Exact statement the asset can support |
| Definitions | Metric, population and reporting window |
| Permission | Who approved which channels and uses |
| Redactions | Information removed and reason |
| Expiry or review | When approval or factual relevance needs checking |

Permission to use a quotation in one case study may not cover paid advertising or an edited endorsement. Keep the intended use explicit.

## Worked example: an impressive dashboard with a private metric

Illustrative example: a client allows a campaign workflow to be described but does not allow monthly revenue publication. The proof asset can show the agreed process, anonymized setup or an authorized statement about the scope. It should not display the private figure in a screenshot, caption, alt text or structured data.

Check downloadable files too. Blurring a thumbnail does not protect the original image if the page links to its unredacted version. Revisit every representation of the asset.

## Match proof strength to claim strength

A testimonial describes someone's experience; it does not prove a universal result. A screenshot shows a recorded state; it may not establish causality. A demonstration shows a mechanism under stated conditions. Use each for the claim it can actually support.

Before publication, have the responsible person verify the asset, permission and wording together. If approval is unclear, use a different proof source rather than inventing an endorsement. Review the [claims guide](before-after-ad-claims.html) for comparisons.

@@ creative-production-capacity
META: Plan creative production using bottleneck capacity, reusable evidence and review time, with a practical workload example for a small marketing team.
ANSWER: Creative production capacity is the amount of usable, approved work a team can produce within its real constraints. Count concept development, evidence gathering, editing and review, not only finished file exports. A faster testing calendar cannot compensate for missing product proof or approval time.
RELATED: creative-portfolio-coverage,growth-team-roles,video-ad-test-plan

## Find the scarce step

Map the path from customer insight to published asset. The bottleneck might be recording product footage, fluent translation or client permission rather than design. Adding more designers will not solve a missing factual review.

Separate a new concept from resizing an approved asset. Reuse can be efficient, but label it accurately. Ten exports of one idea should not become ten claims of creative learning.

## Estimate the production work

| Work | Capacity question |
| Research | Is the customer situation and claim evidence ready? |
| Brief | Can the creator understand one clear task? |
| Production | Are footage, screens and original files available? |
| Review | Who checks facts, language and permissions? |
| Release | Are placement, destination and version records correct? |

Reserve room for rework. A plan that assumes every first draft passes review is fragile.

## Worked example: the calendar exceeds the editor's time

Illustrative example: an editor has twelve usable hours in a week. Three new demonstrations need four hours each before revisions. Scheduling those three plus six cutdowns leaves no space for feedback, caption corrections or urgent fixes.

Choose two demonstrations and the most useful adaptations, or move the release date. The decision should reflect the test question. Producing fewer concepts with accurate proof can be more useful than rushing a broad collection of vague claims.

## Make reuse deliberate

Maintain approved product footage, claim records, fonts and language references. Reuse those materials while changing the buying situation or explanation where the hypothesis requires it. Reuse is not permission to keep an outdated price or an unsupported feature in every new asset.

Track revision causes. Repeated factual corrections indicate a briefing problem; repeated layout fixes indicate an incomplete placement specification. Improve the production process before increasing output targets.

## Set next week's workload

List the available hours in the bottleneck role and the readiness of each concept. Choose work that fills a real gap in the [creative portfolio](creative-portfolio-coverage.html). Include a review owner, release check and source record for every strong claim.
