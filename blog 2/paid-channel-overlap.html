Paid channel overlap: stop adding two platforms’ revenue totals

Understand paid channel overlap using a shared-order example, reconciliation worksheet and a clear distinction between attribution and incremental impact.

Ayoub’s Growth Notebook2 min readPrepared 13 October 2026AI-assisted guide
The short answerPaid channel overlap occurs when several channels receive credit for the same customer or sale. Their attributed revenue totals are not necessarily additive. Use business records to count actual outcomes, then treat platform reports as views of the journey rather than separate revenue ledgers.

Start with one business outcome ledger

Choose a source for completed orders or collected sales and identify each outcome once. Define refunds, taxes, shipping, currency and timezones consistently. A campaign click, payment return and completed purchase should not become three sales in the ledger.

Advertising platforms can use different attribution rules and windows. A person may discover the offer in one channel and search for the brand later. Both platforms may legitimately describe a touchpoint, but their claims do not create two purchases.

Worked example: two claims, one order

Illustrative example: a customer sees a social ad, later clicks a search ad and completes a $100 order. Social and search each report $100 under their respective attribution settings. The business has one $100 order, not $200 revenue.

RecordAmountWhat it means
Business order ledger$100One recorded customer purchase
Social report$100Credit under social attribution rules
Search report$100Credit under search attribution rules
Added platform total$200Overlapping claims, not actual collected revenue

The example is deliberately simple. Actual reconciliation also needs to consider delayed conversions, modeled data, currencies and refunds. Different totals do not automatically mean one system is malfunctioning.

Separate reporting credit from added impact

Attribution asks how credit is assigned among observed touches. Incrementality asks what would have happened without an intervention. A branded search click can receive credit even when the customer already intended to purchase. It can also play a useful role. The report alone cannot settle which explanation applies.

Use controlled evidence where feasible, such as a suitable holdout or geo test. Choose a design that changes the exposure being investigated and measures business outcomes. Do not describe an arbitrary attribution rule as proof of causal contribution.

Build a reconciliation view

Report actual orders, channel-attributed outcomes and unresolved differences in separate columns. Add the window and definition beside each number. Use a consistent acquisition cohort when comparing new customers, and do not merge acquisition and repeat-purchase outcomes without saying so.

Investigate extreme discrepancies with known transaction IDs or permitted matching records. Missing purchases, duplicate events and self-referrals require a tracking fix. Ordinary differences caused by attribution rules require an explanation rather than forcing all systems to agree.

Change the budget conversation

Ask whether the next channel investment creates additional contribution, serves a distinct customer task or protects an important part of the journey. Avoid reallocating solely because one platform claims more revenue. The holdout testing guide helps frame the added-impact question.
