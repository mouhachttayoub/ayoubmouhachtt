Situation / Context
Start with the business problem.

A SaaS product with real value — but no acquisition architecture.

eGrow had a working B2B SaaS product and a real market, but customer acquisition was fragmented across cold email, sporadic Google Ads and word of mouth.

The brief was to build the acquisition and activation engine from the ground up and move the business toward $17K in monthly recurring revenue within 90 days.

The diagnostic work showed that the biggest growth constraint was not traffic. It was the combination of vague ICP definition, slow activation and unsystematic outbound.

Diagnosis
Find the leak before buying more traffic.

Three failures were suppressing growth.

01

No sharp ICP

The team was marketing to multiple e-commerce segments at once. The strongest LTV signal came from Shopify brands importing from Asia, so the acquisition system was rebuilt around that segment.

02

14-day time-to-value

Users were waiting too long before reaching the supplier-integration moment that made the product valuable. Onboarding friction was causing churn before activation.

03

No repeatable outbound

Cold outreach had no targeting logic, sequence or follow-up system. Leads existed, but there was no reliable connection between outreach and revenue.

Architecture
The system built.

Four workstreams. One commercial outcome.

01

ICP + messaging

Defined the highest-LTV customer profile and rebuilt messaging around supplier pain rather than product features.

02

Onboarding redesign

Removed friction before supplier integration and created a quick-win path that moved activation from day 14 to day 3.

03

Cold outbound engine

Built a structured Apollo-powered sequence with pain-led messaging, case proof and clear demo conversion logic.

04

Search + retargeting

Rebuilt Google Search around bottom-of-funnel intent and used LinkedIn retargeting to reinforce proof for high-intent visitors.

Signal / Learning
What the case actually proved.

The biggest lever was onboarding — not more ad spend.

Acquisition cannot compensate for a product experience that loses users before they feel the value. The highest-return work happened below the top of the funnel.

ICP specificity also multiplied every channel. When the message, search intent and outbound list all described the same high-value customer, the system became easier to optimize.

“Fix retention before you scale acquisition.”

Your SaaS may not need more traffic. It may need a cleaner growth architecture.
