@@ ecommerce-size-guide-conversion
META: Improve an e-commerce size guide with useful measurements, fit context and consistent product information, then evaluate returns as well as purchases.
ANSWER: A useful size guide helps a customer choose the right variant for their actual measurements and fit preferences. It should explain units and how to measure, not simply display unexplained size labels. Evaluate suitable purchases and size-related returns together.
RELATED: ecommerce-return-reason-insights,ecommerce-product-page-copy,ecommerce-product-comparison-table

## Identify the questions shoppers actually ask

Review product-specific fit questions and permitted return records. Customers may need to know garment measurements, body measurements or compatibility dimensions. Those are different things. Label the basis of each measurement clearly.

Use measurements from the actual product or supported manufacturer record. Do not apply one chart to every item because the size labels look similar. State meaningful tolerances or variation only when the information is available and accurate.

## Build a usable guide

| Element | Question it answers |
| Measurement basis | Is this the product or the customer's body? |
| Units | Are values in centimeters, inches or both? |
| Method | Where and how should the measurement be taken? |
| Fit context | What does a close or relaxed fit mean for this item? |
| Boundary advice | What should someone between sizes consider? |
| Support path | How can the customer resolve an uncertain fit? |

Avoid guaranteed-fit claims unless the offer genuinely supports them. A clearer guide reduces uncertainty; it cannot eliminate individual variation.

## Worked example: the label hides the measurement

Illustrative example: a shopper normally buys size M, but the product's M differs from another brand's. A chart with only S, M and L does not answer the question. A clearly labeled measurement table and simple measurement instructions provide a more useful comparison.

If customer photos or model measurements are used, obtain permission and keep the details accurate. Do not present an edited image as reliable measurement evidence.

## Check the whole product page

Keep the guide near the variant decision and understandable on mobile. Verify that the chosen size, stock state and cart line match. A correct chart cannot repair a checkout that selects a different variant.

Review size-related support, returns and contribution after changes. A higher immediate conversion rate may be undesirable if more buyers choose incorrectly. Start with one product family and compare mature outcomes using the [return analysis guide](ecommerce-return-reason-insights.html).

@@ ecommerce-product-comparison-table
META: Build product comparison tables around meaningful customer choices, verified attributes and mobile readability instead of a long feature checklist.
ANSWER: A product comparison table should help shoppers choose between genuinely different options. Compare attributes that affect their task, state units and explain unfamiliar terms. A table that makes every option look identical creates more information without a clearer decision.
RELATED: ecommerce-size-guide-conversion,competitive-alternative-map,ecommerce-product-page-copy

## Start with the choosing task

Ask why a customer would select one option over another: size, supported use, compatibility, capacity or an included service. Avoid rows that exist only because the seller can list them. “Premium quality” is not a verifiable comparison dimension by itself.

Use actual product specifications and update them when the product changes. Distinguish missing information from an absent feature. Do not invent a competitor attribute to make your preferred option appear better.

## Choose a concise set of rows

| Row type | Useful treatment |
| Fit or dimensions | Include units and measurement basis |
| Supported task | Explain the practical use case |
| Included items | Separate the purchase from optional accessories |
| Compatibility | State actual supported conditions |
| Price | Use the same currency and billing basis |
| Availability | Keep the stock or delivery condition current |

The comparison should be readable without interpreting a wall of unexplained checkmarks.

## Worked example: three storage containers

Illustrative example: three containers differ in usable capacity, dimensions and closure type. A shopper selecting one for a shelf needs external dimensions; a shopper storing a measured quantity needs usable capacity. Include both clearly rather than ranking every container as “best.”

State which option fits which situation. If you show props in images, distinguish them from included items. The table and gallery should describe the same purchase.

## Review on a small screen

Preserve headers while allowing a readable comparison, and make horizontal scrolling apparent where used. Do not shrink text until it becomes unreadable. Keep important conditions visible in the product descriptions as well.

Evaluate product-selection questions, mistaken orders and returns alongside conversion. A useful table may move buyers toward a less expensive but better-fitting option. That can improve the customer experience even when average basket value falls.

@@ ecommerce-subscription-fit
META: Evaluate e-commerce subscription fit through recurring need, replenishment timing, customer control and contribution before offering a recurring plan.
ANSWER: An e-commerce subscription fits when customers have a recurring need and the plan serves it reliably. Repeat purchases alone do not prove they want a fixed schedule. Check timing, flexibility, fulfillment costs and the clarity of the recurring commitment.
RELATED: ecommerce-repeat-purchase-rate,renewal-reminder-strategy,ecommerce-inventory-marketing

## Investigate the repeat task

Use actual repeat-order intervals and customer conversations. Separate consumable replenishment from occasional gifts or seasonal purchases. A repeat buyer may want convenient reordering without wanting automatic delivery.

Identify variation in the interval. If customers consume the product at different rates, a fixed monthly schedule can create excess stock for some and shortages for others. Only advertise skip, pause or frequency controls the service actually supports.

## Evaluate the offer's operating conditions

| Condition | Question |
| Recurring need | Does the customer need another delivery on this cadence? |
| Customer control | Can the plan adapt using supported options? |
| Supply | Can the business fulfill recurring commitments? |
| Contribution | Do discounts and repeated shipping leave usable margin? |
| Communication | Are charge, delivery and renewal terms understandable? |

Keep one-time purchase and subscription conditions distinct at checkout.

## Worked example: repeat behavior is irregular

Illustrative example: customers reorder a product after anywhere from three to eight weeks. A monthly subscription may be appropriate for some, but not all. The next investigation is consumption pattern and customer preference, not a blanket recurring plan for every repeat buyer.

A pilot can observe suitable participation, skips where supported, cancellations and contribution. Label the pilot's conditions accurately and do not treat its selected customers as representative of the whole store.

## Review more than subscription sign-ups

Observe fulfilled renewals, complaints, cancellations and variable cost over comparable cohort ages. An initial discount can increase enrollment while producing little continued value. Include discount and servicing costs in the review.

Before launch, ask a new reader to explain what will happen after the first purchase. Repair confusion about timing or commitment. Use the [renewal communication guide](renewal-reminder-strategy.html) to keep recurring charges and available options clear.

@@ ecommerce-return-reason-insights
META: Turn product return reasons into practical improvements while separating recorded reasons, suspected causes, cohort maturity and contribution impact.
ANSWER: Product return analysis connects recorded return reasons to the customer's purchase expectations and the actual product. Keep reasons separate from your interpretation. Use mature cohorts and financial consequences to prioritize improvements rather than treating every return as a marketing failure.
RELATED: ecommerce-size-guide-conversion,cod-product-disclosure,contribution-margin-waterfall

## Make the reason categories useful

Distinguish fit, damage, wrong item, expectation mismatch and changed need where your records support those categories. Preserve free-text detail appropriately, without exposing personal information. A default reason selected because no suitable option exists can distort the analysis.

Look at the product, variant, channel and timing. A campaign can attract customers expecting a feature the product does not have, but warehouse mistakes or transit damage need different owners and responses.

## Match causes to actions

| Recorded pattern | Investigation | Potential response |
| Size mismatch | Measurement and variant choice | Improve the product-specific guide |
| Missing expected item | Offer and image interpretation | Clarify what is included |
| Damage | Packing and delivery handling | Operational repair |
| Wrong variant | Picking and checkout record | Fulfillment or interface correction |
| Changed need | Buying context and timing | Review acquisition fit, without assuming blame |

These are investigation paths, not automatic conclusions.

## Worked example: the gallery creates the wrong expectation

Illustrative example: a digital product preview includes jars, and buyers mistake the purchase for physical packaging. Adding more attractive props does not solve the issue. The main image and description should clearly show the actual digital deliverable and distinguish mockup props.

After the change, compare expectation-related inquiries and mature purchase outcomes. A lower conversion rate can be acceptable if the previous version persuaded unsuitable buyers to buy the wrong thing.

## Include the cost of the return

Refunded revenue, unrecovered product cost, handling and reverse shipping affect contribution differently. Account for recovery or resale consistently. Avoid counting every return as a total loss if the inventory can actually be recovered, or assuming full recovery without evidence.

Compare equally mature cohorts so recent purchases have had time to return. Choose one repeated reason and assign a responsible owner. Track whether the change improves the customer problem, not only the return percentage.

@@ ecommerce-paid-traffic-capacity
META: Check whether your store can fulfill paid traffic using available stock, handling capacity, customer support and delivery promises before scaling spend.
ANSWER: Paid traffic capacity is the demand a store can serve without breaking its promise or economics. It includes inventory, picking, packing, delivery and support. A campaign can perform well in an advertising report while overwhelming the operation that must fulfill its orders.
RELATED: ecommerce-inventory-marketing,marketing-logistics-alignment,ad-spend-cash-flow

## Identify the limiting stage

Map daily order capacity through stock availability, confirmation if needed, packing and dispatch. Distinguish an average capacity from a reliable capacity under the planned product mix. Complex orders may consume more time than simple ones.

Check support coverage as well. Delayed responses about delivery or payment can create canceled orders even when warehouse capacity is sufficient. Record who can restrict exposure when the queue grows.

## Use a readiness worksheet

| Capacity | What to verify |
| Stock | Available units and variant mix |
| Handling | Orders the team can process under current staffing |
| Delivery | Supported destinations and actual dispatch schedule |
| Support | Ability to answer the promised customer channels |
| Cash | Costs incurred before collection |
| Recovery | Plan for incidents and delayed orders |

Do not promise a universal scaling rate. Capacity and demand volatility differ by business.

## Worked example: advertising outpaces packing

Illustrative example: a team reliably dispatches 80 orders per day and already receives 65. A promotion adding 40 daily orders produces 105, exceeding that capacity by 25. If the mismatch lasts four days, the theoretical backlog grows by 100 orders before any recovery work.

The response might be additional verified capacity, a slower campaign or a longer transparently communicated dispatch window. Continuing the old promise while the queue grows creates a preventable customer problem.

## Review the whole cohort

Measure delivered orders, delays, support demand, refunds and contribution after outcomes mature. A low lead or order cost is not enough if fulfillment deteriorates. Compare demand with the operation's current ability to serve it, not a future staffing plan that is not in place.

Before the next increase, write expected additional orders and the capacity response at each limiting stage. Use the [inventory guide](ecommerce-inventory-marketing.html) for stock constraints and the [cash-flow guide](ad-spend-cash-flow.html) for upfront funding.
