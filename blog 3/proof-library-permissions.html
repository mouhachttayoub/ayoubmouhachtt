Build a marketing proof library without leaking client information

Organize testimonials, dashboards and demonstrations with publication permissions, source records and confidentiality checks before using them in marketing.

Ayoub’s Growth Notebook2 min readPrepared 13 October 2026AI-assisted guide
The short answerA marketing proof library should store the original evidence, the claim it supports and permission for each intended use. Public proof is a selected, authorized view of the record. Never assume access to a dashboard means its figures can be published.

Keep evidence and public assets separate

Retain the original record in an appropriately restricted location. Create a publication version that removes unnecessary identifying or confidential information. Redaction should protect privacy without changing the meaning of the evidence.

Do not replace a private number with an invented public result. If a revenue figure cannot be disclosed, explain the work, process and permitted outcomes instead. A specific implementation story can be credible without revealing confidential finances.

Store the permissions with the asset

FieldWhat to retain
SourceOriginal record and date
ClaimExact statement the asset can support
DefinitionsMetric, population and reporting window
PermissionWho approved which channels and uses
RedactionsInformation removed and reason
Expiry or reviewWhen approval or factual relevance needs checking

Permission to use a quotation in one case study may not cover paid advertising or an edited endorsement. Keep the intended use explicit.

Worked example: an impressive dashboard with a private metric

Illustrative example: a client allows a campaign workflow to be described but does not allow monthly revenue publication. The proof asset can show the agreed process, anonymized setup or an authorized statement about the scope. It should not display the private figure in a screenshot, caption, alt text or structured data.

Check downloadable files too. Blurring a thumbnail does not protect the original image if the page links to its unredacted version. Revisit every representation of the asset.

Match proof strength to claim strength

A testimonial describes someone's experience; it does not prove a universal result. A screenshot shows a recorded state; it may not establish causality. A demonstration shows a mechanism under stated conditions. Use each for the claim it can actually support.

Before publication, have the responsible person verify the asset, permission and wording together. If approval is unclear, use a different proof source rather than inventing an endorsement. Review the claims guide for comparisons.
