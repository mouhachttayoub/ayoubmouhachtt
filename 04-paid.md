@@ search-terms-waste-classification
META: Classify search term waste by intent, relevance and commercial fit before changing negatives, with examples and a repeatable review worksheet.
ANSWER: Search term analysis should separate irrelevant searches from relevant searches that performed poorly. Classify intent before excluding traffic. A keyword can bring suitable buyers but still suffer from a weak offer, an unsuitable landing page or incomplete conversion reporting.
RELATED: google-ads-negative-keyword-strategy,google-ads-search-intent,offline-conversion-quality-loop

## Use a classification that leads to an action

Review available search terms alongside the offer they reached. Record the buyer's likely task, evidence of fit and outcome maturity. Reports may not expose every query, so state their coverage rather than treating the visible list as all advertising traffic.

Do not call every non-converting click waste. A suitable buyer may need time to purchase, and a small number of clicks cannot establish a reliable conversion rate. Irrelevance and poor economics are different reasons for a change.

| Classification | Example for a paid reporting tool | Potential action |
| Wrong task | Jobs as a reporting analyst | Exclude the irrelevant intent |
| Wrong product | Printable school report template | Clarify or exclude the unrelated product |
| Relevant research | How to automate client reports | Decide whether education fits the campaign |
| Commercial evaluation | Reporting tool for agencies | Review offer and landing-page fit |
| Existing-customer task | Login to the named product | Separate navigation from acquisition |

The examples are illustrative. A phrase can have several meanings; inspect the surrounding context before assigning a category.

## Work backward from qualified outcomes

Connect queries to later qualification when reliable records exist. A low-cost term producing unsuitable leads is different from an expensive term producing profitable customers. Keep media cost separate from sales effort when assessing the value of demand.

Look for repeated patterns rather than creating a negative from one ambiguous search. Use the appropriate exclusion scope and matching behavior supported by the advertising platform. Check whether the proposed exclusion could block a legitimate product or customer situation.

## Worked example: fix relevance or fix the page?

Illustrative example: searches about “agency reporting software” bring suitable buyers, but the landing page mainly promotes a consumer budget spreadsheet. Adding “agency” as a negative would hide the mismatch by eliminating demand the business intended to serve.

The better investigation is whether the advertised offer and page accurately address agency reporting. By contrast, “reporting analyst salary” is a different task and can justify an intent exclusion. These decisions follow meaning, not whether one click converted.

## Maintain a review log

Record the term category, proposed action, scope, date and reason. Include a reversal path for exclusions that later prove too broad. Revisit the classification when the product or offer changes; yesterday's irrelevant segment can become relevant after a genuine expansion.

Compare outcomes before and after changes using consistent windows. Removing traffic can raise a conversion percentage while reducing valuable customers. Review total qualified outcomes and contribution, not only the new percentage.

## Review the next search-term export

Add columns for intent, fit, maturity and action. Leave uncertain terms marked uncertain. Use the [negative keyword guide](google-ads-negative-keyword-strategy.html) for exclusion principles and the [offline quality loop](offline-conversion-quality-loop.html) when lead quality arrives after the click.

@@ paid-channel-overlap
META: Understand paid channel overlap using a shared-order example, reconciliation worksheet and a clear distinction between attribution and incremental impact.
ANSWER: Paid channel overlap occurs when several channels receive credit for the same customer or sale. Their attributed revenue totals are not necessarily additive. Use business records to count actual outcomes, then treat platform reports as views of the journey rather than separate revenue ledgers.
RELATED: marketing-attribution-vs-incrementality,new-customer-revenue-definition,marketing-data-reconciliation

## Start with one business outcome ledger

Choose a source for completed orders or collected sales and identify each outcome once. Define refunds, taxes, shipping, currency and timezones consistently. A campaign click, payment return and completed purchase should not become three sales in the ledger.

Advertising platforms can use different attribution rules and windows. A person may discover the offer in one channel and search for the brand later. Both platforms may legitimately describe a touchpoint, but their claims do not create two purchases.

## Worked example: two claims, one order

Illustrative example: a customer sees a social ad, later clicks a search ad and completes a $100 order. Social and search each report $100 under their respective attribution settings. The business has one $100 order, not $200 revenue.

| Record | Amount | What it means |
| Business order ledger | $100 | One recorded customer purchase |
| Social report | $100 | Credit under social attribution rules |
| Search report | $100 | Credit under search attribution rules |
| Added platform total | $200 | Overlapping claims, not actual collected revenue |

The example is deliberately simple. Actual reconciliation also needs to consider delayed conversions, modeled data, currencies and refunds. Different totals do not automatically mean one system is malfunctioning.

## Separate reporting credit from added impact

Attribution asks how credit is assigned among observed touches. Incrementality asks what would have happened without an intervention. A branded search click can receive credit even when the customer already intended to purchase. It can also play a useful role. The report alone cannot settle which explanation applies.

Use controlled evidence where feasible, such as a suitable holdout or geo test. Choose a design that changes the exposure being investigated and measures business outcomes. Do not describe an arbitrary attribution rule as proof of causal contribution.

## Build a reconciliation view

Report actual orders, channel-attributed outcomes and unresolved differences in separate columns. Add the window and definition beside each number. Use a consistent acquisition cohort when comparing new customers, and do not merge acquisition and repeat-purchase outcomes without saying so.

Investigate extreme discrepancies with known transaction IDs or permitted matching records. Missing purchases, duplicate events and self-referrals require a tracking fix. Ordinary differences caused by attribution rules require an explanation rather than forcing all systems to agree.

## Change the budget conversation

Ask whether the next channel investment creates additional contribution, serves a distinct customer task or protects an important part of the journey. Avoid reallocating solely because one platform claims more revenue. The [holdout testing guide](holdout-test-design.html) helps frame the added-impact question.

@@ ad-spend-cash-flow
META: Plan ad spend around cash collection, variable costs and operating capacity with a worked funding-gap example and a practical budget worksheet.
ANSWER: An advertising budget should reflect when money leaves the business and when customer cash becomes available. Reported revenue does not fund today's campaign if it has not been collected. Map advertising, inventory, fulfillment and payment timing before increasing spend.
RELATED: cod-cash-cycle,marginal-roas-budget,contribution-margin-waterfall

## Draw the cash timeline

List the dates on which the business pays for ads, product, delivery, tools and service work. Then record when buyers pay and when the business can use those funds. A cash-on-delivery store, invoiced service and prepaid subscription have different timing patterns.

Keep expected cash separate from confirmed cash. Pending orders can be refused, invoices can be delayed and payments can be refunded. Use actual records to estimate these effects rather than assuming every reported conversion funds the next one.

| Budget input | What to record |
| Starting available cash | Funds actually usable for the planned period |
| Advertising payments | Dates and amounts expected to leave |
| Variable operating costs | Costs required to deliver the acquired demand |
| Collections | Expected and confirmed dates, kept distinct |
| Reserve | Room for delays, refunds and unexpected costs |

This is an operating worksheet, not a substitute for the business's financial accounts. Align its definitions with the people responsible for cash management.

## Worked example: profitable on paper, short of cash

Illustrative example: a campaign needs $1,000 of advertising and $1,200 of fulfillment payments before customers' $3,000 becomes available. The contribution after those two costs is $800, excluding other costs. The temporary funding requirement is $2,200 if there are no earlier collections.

Starting with $1,500 creates a $700 gap even though the simplified contribution is positive. The response might be slower acquisition, different payment timing or more available working cash. A revenue screenshot does not resolve that timing problem.

## Model delays rather than one perfect scenario

Consider a later collection date, more refunds or a higher proportion of unresolved orders. Keep the downside assumptions explicit. If a small delay makes payroll or fulfillment impossible, the advertising pace needs to reflect that constraint.

Avoid using uncertain future repeat purchases to justify immediate obligations. They may be commercially valuable, but they do not necessarily arrive in time to cover the next bill. Show immediate cash requirements separately from longer-term customer contribution.

## Connect the spending review to operations

Before a budget increase, confirm stock, support and delivery capacity. Extra demand can require additional upfront expense. Set an owner for monitoring the cash timeline and a limit at which new spending pauses for review.

Track forecast versus actual collections to improve the next planning cycle. Use the [COD cash-cycle guide](cod-cash-cycle.html) when payment depends on delivery and courier remittance. Keep currency and period consistent throughout the worksheet.

@@ paid-experiment-exclusion-log
META: Keep an advertising experiment exclusion log for audience changes, incidents and missing data without removing inconvenient outcomes after the fact.
ANSWER: An exclusion log records what was removed from an experiment, when, why and how it affects interpretation. Define exclusions before results where possible. Removing a bad-looking segment after seeing performance can create a flattering but biased comparison.
RELATED: sample-ratio-mismatch,paid-media-geo-tests,ab-test-results-interpretation

## Distinguish eligibility from post-result cleanup

Eligibility defines who belongs in the experiment: supported regions, new customers, compatible devices or another relevant condition. Document it before exposure. Cleanup addresses problems in the record, such as duplicate transactions or a verified logging incident.

Do not treat a customer's failure to convert as a data error. A treatment that causes people to abandon a page can also cause less downstream logging. Deleting those users would remove part of the effect you need to understand.

## Record every consequential change

| Field | Why it matters |
| Change date | Locates which exposure was affected |
| Rule | Makes the exclusion reproducible |
| Reason and evidence | Separates a verified incident from a preference |
| Affected groups | Shows whether treatment and control changed differently |
| Count or spend | Reveals the size of the impact |
| Interpretation | Explains whether the original question remains answerable |

Include changes to geography, customer status, conversion definitions, creative availability and tracking. An exclusion that seems small can matter if it affects a high-value group disproportionately.

## Worked example: the page-speed incident

Illustrative example: a new landing page fails to load for some mobile visitors. The team removes those visits from the treatment data and reports a higher conversion rate among the remaining visitors. That describes successful loads, not the full customer effect of the page.

The primary review should retain the affected eligible population when estimating the customer experience, while documenting the incident. A separate diagnostic view of successful loads can help locate the issue. Label the two views clearly rather than calling the filtered result the experiment's overall lift.

## Use sensitivity analysis honestly

When a justified correction changes the result, show the original and corrected calculations with their definitions. Explain why the correction is appropriate. If uncertainty remains about missing records, present a range or mark the result inconclusive.

Ask whether an exclusion could be influenced by the treatment. For example, excluding users who did not finish onboarding can hide a treatment that harmed onboarding. The analysis population should follow the question, not the outcome the team wishes to report.

## Keep the log with the decision

Store the experiment brief, changes, results and final action together. Include the log in any handover so another person can reproduce the reasoning. For website tests, perform a [sample ratio mismatch check](sample-ratio-mismatch.html) before interpreting conversion differences.

@@ agency-handover-checklist
META: Use a marketing agency handover checklist covering account access, campaign decisions, creative rights, measurement and operational continuity.
ANSWER: A useful agency handover transfers access, current decisions and the evidence behind them. Exporting campaign screenshots is not enough. The receiving team needs to know what is running, why it is running and which dependencies could interrupt customers or reporting.
RELATED: growth-team-roles,marketing-data-quality-contract,proof-library-permissions

## Inventory the assets the business relies on

List advertising accounts, analytics properties, landing pages, domains, tags, creative files, product feeds and integrations. Verify ownership and access through the actual platform settings. Keep credentials out of public documents and avoid sharing accounts when a platform supports individual permissions.

Distinguish access from ownership. An agency may have permission to manage an account without owning it. Record the administrative owner and the person responsible for removing access after a verified transfer. Do not revoke the old team's access before the new team confirms continuity.

## Transfer decisions with their context

| Item | Minimum useful handover |
| Live campaigns | Objective, audience, budget and current constraints |
| Experiments | Hypothesis, comparison, status and unresolved questions |
| Creative | Editable files, claim sources and usage permissions |
| Measurement | Event definitions, known gaps and reconciliation process |
| Operations | Approval contacts, lead routing and customer follow-up |
| Next review | Pending decisions and the dates outcomes mature |

Include failures and discontinued tests. Otherwise the receiving team may spend the first month relearning why a plausible idea did not work.

## Worked example: the missing automation owner

Illustrative example: an incoming marketer receives advertising access but not the integration that routes demo leads. Campaigns keep spending while inquiries stop reaching sales. A proper handover would trace one test inquiry from ad to form, CRM and assigned owner before access changes.

The check should include customer confirmations and suppression after cancellation. A technically successful form submission can still create a poor experience if the customer gets no next step or several conflicting messages.

## Review creative and proof permissions

Confirm which files can be edited, where they can be used and whether testimonial or dashboard permissions still apply. Do not assume every agency-created asset carries unrestricted usage rights. Keep confidential client information out of the handover copy used for public portfolios.

Record the factual sources behind strong claims. “This creative won” should include the outcome definition, period and limitations. A platform screenshot without maturity or audience context is not enough to justify recreating the same campaign.

## Complete the transfer with a readiness check

Have the receiving team verify access, billing continuity, one complete customer journey and the next reporting cycle. Then confirm the agreed access changes and ownership record. Use the [data quality contract](marketing-data-quality-contract.html) when event definitions are unclear, and the [proof library guide](proof-library-permissions.html) for sensitive assets.
