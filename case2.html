Situation / Context
Start with the business problem.

A mobile app with strong retention signals — and no paid acquisition infrastructure.

The product had organic traction, but the previous paid acquisition setup had burned roughly $40K with CPI above $6.80 and no reliable path to positive ROAS.

The objective was to build a system capable of reaching 1M active users in 120 days while proving the economics before scaling aggressively.

The key issue was not simply media buying. It was a combination of weak creative operations, broad optimization and broken attribution.

Diagnosis
Find the leak before buying more traffic.

The old campaign was scaling before it was ready.

01

No creative testing system

Three creatives were left running for months. Fatigue compounded, CPM increased and CPI kept rising because there was no structured way to discover new winners.

02

Broad audience optimization

The campaigns optimized for cheap installs rather than retained users. The algorithm was finding volume, not quality.

03

Broken attribution

Post-ATT measurement relied too heavily on platform reporting. The team could not see which creatives and channels were producing retained, valuable users.

Architecture
The system built.

Infrastructure first. Scale second.

01

Attribution infrastructure

Integrated an MMP, server-side events and retention signals so campaign decisions could be tied to user quality instead of installs alone.

02

Creative testing engine

Built a weekly sprint model testing multiple hook families with explicit kill thresholds and a clear path from experiment to scale.

03

Meta + Google UAC scaling

Moved winning creative into larger campaigns and optimized around retained-user signals.

04

Apple Search + reactivation

Added search-intent capture and a retargeting loop for users who installed but had not completed onboarding.

Signal / Learning
What the case actually proved.

Mobile growth behaves like a creative operations system.

Creative quality explained far more of the performance variance than audience micro-targeting. The advantage came from producing and evaluating new hooks quickly.

The other major lesson was measurement: cheap installs are not necessarily good users. The optimization target must move deeper into retention and monetization.

“CPI is a vanity metric. Retained-user cost is the real number.”
